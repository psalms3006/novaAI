# NOVA Beta Program

## Operating principle

Low friction. High engagement. Real feedback.

This is a lightweight beta program. It is designed to be run by a founder or small team using WhatsApp and a form. No project manager. No dashboards. No certifications.

The tester experience should feel like early access to something exciting — not an unpaid job.

---

## 1. Beta Application

### Goal

Get someone from "interested" to "using NOVA" in under 60 seconds.

### Form: "Join the NOVA Beta"

**Header:**
NOVA Beta — Early Access
"Help us shape the future of voice-first AI on the desktop."

**Fields:**

1. Name — text
2. Email — text
3. WhatsApp number — text (+country code)
4. Occupation / student status — text (optional)
5. Operating system — single select (Windows 10, Windows 11, macOS, Linux)
6. RAM — single select (Under 8 GB, 8–16 GB, 16 GB+)
7. How often do you use AI assistants? — single select (Rarely, Sometimes, Daily, Heavy AI user)
8. What would you most like to use NOVA for? — multi-select (Studying/research, Coding, Productivity, Computer automation, Voice conversations, Writing, Personal assistant, Exploring AI, Other)
9. Why would you like to try NOVA? — text (optional)

**Submit button:** "Join the Beta"

**Confirmation screen (immediate, after submit):**

> You're in.
> Join the NOVA Beta Lab.
> [WhatsApp button/link]
> [Download NOVA link]
> We'll send your first mission there.

### Form rules

- Estimated completion: 1 minute.
- Do NOT ask for detailed hardware specs.
- Do NOT ask testers to rate NOVA before using it.
- Do NOT include long consent questionnaires.
- Do NOT make this feel like employment.

## 2. NOVA Beta Lab (WhatsApp Group)

### Name

**NOVA Beta Lab**

### Positioning

This is the beta environment. It is informal, active, and developer-connected.

It is not a social community. It is a working group of early users and the NOVA team.

### What happens here

- Early access to new builds
- Sharing experiences
- Reporting bugs
- Suggesting features
- Discussing what feels good / bad
- Giving developers direct insight into real usage

### Tone

Informal. Direct. Human.

Testers should feel comfortable saying:

- "NOVA misunderstood me here."
- "This is actually really cool."
- "The response was too slow."
- "I asked it to do X and it did Y."
- "The agent visualization is sick."
- "The voice sounds weird."

Do not force every observation into a form. Casual WhatsApp feedback is valuable.

### Group rules

1. Be respectful and constructive.
2. Share bugs and feedback freely.
3. Use the bug form only when a developer asks you to — casual reports stay in chat unless we need structure.
4. No off-topic discussion.
5. Keep pre-release builds and screenshots inside the group. Do not share publicly.

### Developer presence

At least one NOVA developer should be active in the group daily during the beta. The group fails if it becomes a ghost town.

## 3. Onboarding Flow

### Step 1: Application submitted

System sends confirmation email:

> You're in.
> Join the NOVA Beta Lab: [link]
> Download NOVA: [link]
> Your first mission is waiting in the group.

### Step 2: Tester joins WhatsApp

Developer sends a pinned welcome message:

> Welcome to NOVA Beta Lab.
>
> This is where we test new builds, talk about what works, and fix what doesn't.
>
> Your first step: install NOVA and say something to it.
>
> Then tell us what you noticed. Good, bad, weird — it all helps.

### Step 3: First mission (implicit)

The first mission is implicit: install NOVA, have one conversation, share what you noticed.

Do not overwhelm new testers with formal instructions.

### Step 4: First feedback loop

Developer reads the first messages. Responds personally. Fixes urgent issues. Announces the next build.

## 4. Testing Missions

### Philosophy

Missions are occasional challenges, not daily homework.

They should take 5–15 minutes. They should feel like a challenge from a friend, not a QA ticket.

### Frequency

- New build → optional light mission
- Major feature → focused mission
- Otherwise → no mission. Just use NOVA and talk about it.

### Format

Post in WhatsApp as a short, casual challenge.

#### Example: Voice Mission

> 🎙️ VOICE TEST
>
> Talk to NOVA for 5 minutes without touching the keyboard.
>
> Try:
> - Interrupting NOVA
> - Speaking quickly
> - Changing topics
> - Asking follow-up questions
>
> Tell us what feels natural and what doesn't.

#### Example: Research Mission

> 🔍 RESEARCH TEST
>
> Ask NOVA to research something you actually care about.
>
> Don't test it — use it.
>
> Tell us: did the answer help you?

#### Example: Stress Mission

> 🤯 STRESS TEST
>
> Try to confuse NOVA.
>
> Ask it to do something ambiguous, impossible, or contradictory.
>
> Tell us how it reacts.

#### Example: Offline Mission

> 📴 OFFLINE TEST
>
> Disconnect wifi.
> Continue using NOVA.
>
> Tell us: what broke, what worked, and how it felt.

### Mission rules

- Maximum one mission per week.
- Missions are optional. Casual testing is always welcome.
- Do not grade testers on mission completion.
- Do not require mission reports. WhatsApp conversation is enough.

## 5. Feedback Forms

### Philosophy

Do not ask for feedback after every session. That creates fatigue.

Collect structured feedback only at key moments:

- After first use (implicit in onboarding)
- Week 1 check-in
- Major build release
- Feature-specific feedback

### Cadence

- **First 24 hours:** WhatsApp conversation only. No form.
- **Week 1:** One short check-in form.
- **Per major build:** Optional short form.
- **Feature-specific:** Only when NOVA team needs structured input on a specific area.

### Feedback Form

**Title:** "NOVA Beta Check-In"
**Estimated time:** 2 minutes.

**Fields:**

1. How would you rate NOVA right now? — 1–5 stars
2. What's the best thing about NOVA? — text (required)
3. What's the most frustrating thing? — text (required)
4. What broke or didn't work? — text (optional)
5. What should we improve next? — text (required)
6. Would you use NOVA again? — single select (Definitely, Probably, Maybe, Probably not, No)
7. Anything else? — text (optional)

### Conditional logic

- If the tester reports a specific bug or crash, ask: "Want to submit a short bug report so we can track it?"
- If the tester rates 1–2 on overall, ask: "What is the single biggest problem right now?"
- Otherwise, keep it short.

### Form rules

- Do not send this form more than once per 2 weeks.
- Do not require it.
- Do not guilt-trip testers for skipping it.

## 6. Bug Reporting

### Philosophy

Casual bug reports in WhatsApp are the primary input.

Formal bug reports are only requested when the team needs to track a specific issue.

### Flow

1. Tester says in WhatsApp: "NOVA crashed when I asked it to open Chrome."
2. Developer replies: "Can you reproduce it? What exactly did you say?"
3. If reproducible, developer says: "Mind submitting a short bug report so we don't lose track of it?"
4. Tester submits a short bug form.
5. Developer confirms receipt and may ask follow-up questions in WhatsApp.

### Bug Report Form

**Title:** "NOVA Bug Report"
**Estimated time:** 1–2 minutes.

**Fields:**

1. NOVA version — text (e.g., 0.3.1-beta)
2. What were you trying to do? — text
3. What did you expect? — text
4. What actually happened? — text
5. Can you reproduce it? — single select (Always, Sometimes, Once, Don't know)
6. Screenshot/video — optional file upload
7. Severity — single select (Minor, Annoying, Major, Critical)

### Rules

- Do not force this form. Ask for it only when needed.
- Do not require logs unless absolutely necessary.
- Keep it conversational.

## 7. Group Management (Developer Side)

### Daily

- Read WhatsApp messages.
- Respond to bugs, questions, and feedback.
- Thank people for reports.
- Fix obvious issues quickly and announce fixes.

### Weekly

- Post one optional testing mission (5–15 minutes).
- Send Week 1 / periodic feedback form if needed.
- Review bug reports and prioritize fixes.
- Announce new build if available.

### Per New Build

Announcement template:

> NOVA v0.X.X is live.
>
> This build improves:
> - [Voice startup]
> - [Response speed]
> - [Agent visualization]
>
> Use NOVA normally and tell us what you notice.
>
> Especially interested in: [specific thing]
>
> [Download link]

### Developer presence

If the developer goes silent, the beta dies.

The developer should post regularly — even just "Hey, working on voice latency, thanks for the reports today."

## 8. Internal Tracking

### Goal

Track enough to know who is active and what matters. No dashboards, no analytics, no complexity.

### Tool

A simple spreadsheet or table. One row per tester.

### Fields

- Name
- Contact (email / WhatsApp)
- OS / device
- Interests (from application)
- Joined date
- Last active
- Builds tested (list or count)
- Feedback submitted (count)
- Bugs reported (count)
- Status: Active / Inactive / Core Tester / Removed

### Rules

- Update manually. Do not automate this.
- Review monthly.
- Inactive = no messages or activity for 30+ days.

## 9. Core Testers

### When to identify

After the first 4–6 weeks of the beta.

### Who becomes a Core Tester

Not the person with the most reports.

The person who:
- Actually uses NOVA
- Gives honest, specific feedback
- Explains problems clearly
- Reproduces bugs when asked
- Notices UX issues others miss
- Tests new builds promptly
- Suggests useful improvements

### What Core Testers get

- Direct message access to the developer
- First access to new builds
- Recognition as early NOVA testers
- No false promises about rewards, jobs, or money

### How to invite

Send a personal WhatsApp message:

> You've been one of the most useful testers so far.
> Want to become a Core Tester?
>
> Core Testers get early builds and a direct line to us.
> No extra obligations — just keep using NOVA and telling us what you think.

## 10. Feedback → Development Loop

### The loop

1. Tester uses NOVA
2. Tester shares feedback in WhatsApp (casual or formal)
3. Developer reads and responds
4. Developer identifies patterns and problems
5. Developer fixes issues
6. New build released
7. Developer announces build in Beta Lab
8. Testers try new build
9. Testers share new feedback
10. Repeat

### Key principles

- Respond to feedback publicly when possible.
- Announce fixes. "Thanks to @tester for reporting this — it's fixed in v0.X.X."
- Close the loop. If someone reports a bug, tell them when it is fixed.
- Do not over-communicate. Build quality speaks for itself.

---

## 11. Tester Value Exchange

### What testers get

- Early access to NOVA
- Ability to influence development
- Direct communication with the developers
- Opportunity to see their suggestions implemented
- Access to future builds
- Recognition as early NOVA testers

### What testers do NOT get

- Money
- Jobs
- Certificates
- Partnerships
- Guaranteed rewards

Be honest about this from the start.

---

## 12. Anti-Patterns

Do NOT:

- Send long surveys after every session
- Require daily testing
- Make testers feel like employees
- Use corporate QA language
- Create complicated tracking systems
- Force bug reports for every issue
- Ignore casual feedback
- Go silent for weeks
- Over-promise rewards or recognition
- Collect unnecessary personal data

Do:

- Keep forms short
- Make missions optional and fun
- Respond personally to feedback
- Fix things quickly
- Thank testers publicly
- Be honest about what is beta and what isn't

## Summary

The NOVA Beta Program is simple:

1. Short application
2. Join WhatsApp
3. Use NOVA
4. Talk about it
5. Occasional light missions
6. Periodic 2-minute check-ins
7. Fix things
8. Repeat

The goal is not to build a testing organization.

The goal is to get real people using NOVA, talking about it, and helping it get better.
